MoltenVK Enshrouded v19: source audit and corrections
====================================================

Audit performed 2026-10-26; publication updated 2026-10-27.
Hardware: Apple M5 Max, macOS 27.0.1, athei Wine arm64-11.20-3.

RESULT AND SCOPE

Reviewed every source delta between nivogli's published enshrouded-v19
snapshot and its official Khronos upstream base: 15 commits, 14 files,
664 added lines and 40 removed lines. No evidence of a backdoor or added
exfiltration, credential access, process execution, persistence, or hidden
download behavior was found in those changes. This is a bounded source
review and targeted GPU validation, not a proof of security or a complete
audit of Wine, upstream MoltenVK, its dependencies, or the Apple toolchain.

The fork is an experimental Enshrouded performance build. It is not fully
Vulkan-correct under all workloads. A reproduced persistent draw-truncation
bug was fixed locally. Two further synchronization/hoisting corrections
were included. The performance profile still deliberately trades rendering
and numerical guarantees for speed; the remaining limitations are below.

COMPLETE FILE COVERAGE

Paths below are relative to MoltenVK/MoltenVK/. Reproduce the reviewed delta:

  git diff 5189f8de86ec365860f63bf1da736815cfaea5cd..96793f1de5fd898a8e4ad3b7d7b52f23b8bac483

The correction is commit 90d09291a422c572841a189288ff98b838e68d86.
Original nivogli commits and their authorship are preserved in this branch.

1. Commands/MVKCmdDraw.h
   Adds indirect-count hoist hooks and a record-time capped-state flag.
   The latter participated in the reproduced command-reuse bug.

2. Commands/MVKCmdDraw.mm
   Adds count-conversion statistics, draw caps, pre-render conversion,
   reflected tessellation-buffer sizing, vertex budgets, and completion
   diagnostics. Reviewed allocation sizes, dispatch/draw loop counts,
   indexed and non-indexed paths, and buffer ownership. Fixed cap selection
   so reused commands can adapt; matched hoisted allocations to their
   selected count. Tessellation limits remain intentionally lossy.

3. Commands/MVKCmdPipeline.h
   Adds a barrier hook for the indirect-conversion scan. Locally extended
   the stopping rule to execution of secondary command buffers.

4. Commands/MVKCmdPipeline.mm
   Tests whether barriers stop hoisting and keeps eligible compute
   barriers in an open encoder. The fork strips TOP/BOTTOM stage bits in
   this optimization path, but omitted the equivalent fallback fix.
   Locally completed it using the published synchronization-scope fix.

5. Commands/MVKCmdRendering.h
   Marks dynamic-rendering begin/end boundaries for hoisting. Classic
   render-pass beginnings are intentionally excluded because they apply
   external dependencies. No swapchain/HDR presentation behavior changes.

6. Commands/MVKCommand.h
   Adds virtual hooks for identifying boundaries and hoistable commands.
   No external I/O or executable-code loading is introduced.

7. Commands/MVKCommandBuffer.h
   Adds temporary conversion lookup and deferred-barrier state. Locally
   changed each lookup value to include the selected draw count, preventing
   a later cap increase from mismatching an earlier allocation.

8. Commands/MVKCommandBuffer.mm
   Adds the scan ahead of dynamic rendering, compute memory barriers,
   deferred fences for later encoders, and concurrent application compute
   dispatch. Internal compute work retains its existing dispatch modes.
   GPU tests cover dependent compute work followed by a transfer/readback;
   they do not constitute exhaustive Vulkan synchronization conformance.

9. Commands/MVKCommandPipelineStateFactoryShaderSource.h
   Adds GPU atomic counters and tessellation draw clamping. Counter buffer
   slots were checked against the CPU headers. Tessellation product
   accounting uses 64-bit arithmetic before clamping. The clamp prevents
   exceeding the selected scratch budget by reducing work, so an oversized
   draw can lose vertices or instances rather than render in full.

10. GPUObjects/MVKPerfICount.h (new)
    Environment parsing, a small shared GPU statistics buffer, a mutex,
    adaptive cap, histograms, and stderr diagnostics. No remote telemetry.
    GPU/CPU statistics are approximate and not fully synchronized. The
    maximum is measured after capping, and therefore is not the true
    uncapped maximum. The decay floor can preserve an overshot limit.

11. GPUObjects/MVKPerfTess.h (new)
    Validates the configured vertex budget, owns another small shared
    statistics buffer, and emits throttled stderr diagnostics. Both new
    statistics buffers are process-global and retained for process life;
    this design is not robust for multiple Metal devices. This Mac uses
    one Apple GPU. No credential or network behavior was found.

12. GPUObjects/MVKPipeline.h
    Adds reflected buffer-size fields, bounded reflection index storage,
    and stride accessors with fallback values.

13. GPUObjects/MVKPipeline.mm
    Requests Metal reflection, combines producer/consumer sizes, rounds
    accepted sizes to 16 bytes, and falls back for unknown/implausible
    sizes. Reviewed callback locking and lifetime against upstream's
    compiler pattern. This is a sensible memory-saving approach, but the
    full tessellation shader space was not GPU-conformance-tested here.

14. GPUObjects/MVKShaderModule.mm
    Removes optnone from selected SPIRV-Cross precise-math helpers outside
    detected vertex shaders, optionally rewrites precise:: calls to fast::,
    and adds math-mode overrides. These are transparent source transforms,
    not obfuscated payloads. They relax numerical requirements and depend
    on generated MSL formatting. They are inappropriate as a blanket
    general-purpose Vulkan correctness guarantee.

LOCAL CORRECTIONS AND EVIDENCE

A. Reused command buffers could remain capped forever — fixed, reproduced.
   The fork permanently replaced the application's maximum at recording
   time. Increasing a global limit did not restore that saved maximum.
   A GPU probe records one command buffer with 256 real draws, application
   maximum 1024, and starting cap 128, then submits it six times.

   Pristine v19 actual draws: 128, 128, 128, 128, 128, 128.
   Corrected actual draws:   128, 128, 256, 256, 256, 256.
   Uncapped actual draws:    256, 256, 256, 256, 256, 256.

   The correction retains the application maximum and evaluates the cap
   during encoding. A hoisted conversion stores its own count alongside
   its allocation so later cap changes cannot enlarge its consumer loop.
   Indexed, non-indexed, and hoisting-disabled variants passed. The final
   installed binary also passed through athei 11.20-3 Wine/FEX using a
   separate scratch prefix and the same per-executable driver selection.

   PASS in these probe logs means eventual recovery and the checked compute
   dependency result. It does NOT mean the first capped submissions render
   all requested draws. Temporary truncation remains inherent in this
   feedback-based cap; it can recur when a later scene exceeds the limit.

B. Secondary command buffers were not a hoist boundary — guarded locally.
   A secondary buffer can contain a dependency that the primary scan does
   not inspect. The scan now stops there, leaving subsequent conversions
   in their original position. This is a conservative code-review fix;
   there is no dedicated GPU reproducer for this boundary in the evidence.

C. TOP_OF_PIPE/BOTTOM_OF_PIPE fix was only partial — completed locally.
   Applied the published PR 2891 semantics to the fallback barrier path
   and GPUObjects/MVKRenderPass.mm subpass dependencies. TOP in the source
   scope and BOTTOM in the destination scope add no stages. The fork had
   applied that rule only in its inline-compute optimization. The compute
   probe exercises these flags, including a later copy/readback; subpass
   coverage is source review and compilation, not a dedicated GPU test.

REMAINING LIMITATIONS / HISTORICAL MOLTENVK PROFILE

The tested MoltenVK probe configuration and historical per-game launcher used:
  MVK_CONFIG_FAST_MATH_ENABLED=1
  MVK_PERF_FAST_TRANSCENDENTALS=1
  MVK_PERF_ICOUNT_MAX_DRAWS=128
  MVK_PERF_TESS_INDIRECT_VERTS=262144
The branch's C2/C3/C2-wide/C10 and precise-helper optimization defaults
remain enabled. The optional MVK_PERF_MATH_MODE override is not enabled.

The draw cap can briefly omit visible work. MVK_PERF_ICOUNT_MAX_DRAWS=0 disables it
without changing shader precision or the tessellation budget, and may
reduce performance. It is not a fully strict/conformant profile.
The tessellation budget can permanently truncate any draw exceeding it.
The stderr diagnostics report clamps but do not automatically grow it.
Fast math may change NaN/Inf/signed-zero, contraction, and precision behavior.
Nivogli's reported performance and visual assessment were for Enshrouded
on his M5 Pro with HDR off. No local game FPS, visual comparison, Blueprint
stress test, or long gameplay stability test was performed in this task.

HDR INVESTIGATION

The fork does not change upstream swapchain, CAMetalLayer, colorspace, or
drawable-presentation code. Upstream already schedules drawable.present
in a command-buffer scheduled handler. Apple's command-buffer present
convenience API uses that same mechanism, so simply swapping APIs has no
established benefit here.

The existing game log showed HDR RGB10A2/PQ, three swapchain images, and
IMMEDIATE presentation. It already used the commonly suggested triple
buffering configuration. That log describes the prior KosmicKrisp run,
not a new MoltenVK game run.

A standalone native Metal window tested the same scheduled-present pattern
on the 120 Hz display, using 2/3 drawable buffers, SDR/HDR, and VSync off/on.
It did not launch the game. Each case ran 240 frames and discarded warmup
and tail samples. HDR used RGB10A2, PQ colorspace, and extended dynamic
range. The probe is small and lightly loaded; it does not recreate Wine's
fullscreen window, Enshrouded's rendering load, or full HDR brightness.
Reported EDR headroom also varied between runs (1.0 and 1.2).

First pass: HDR/VSync-off late intervals (>1.5 refreshes) were 3 with two
buffers and 1 with three. However, a paired HDR-only repeat in 3,2,2,3 order
produced 3,21,32,18 late intervals. Median GPU-completion-to-presentation
delay varied from roughly 3–20 ms in that repeat. This does not establish
a stable triple-buffering remedy or isolate an HDR-only driver defect.

VSync-on cases had no late intervals in the short initial test and an
8.333 ms 95th-percentile interval, but about 23–27 ms median delay from
GPU completion to presentation. Scheduled-handler delays were around
0.1–0.23 ms at the 95th percentile, not an obvious full-refresh bottleneck
in this probe. No drawable acquisition failures occurred.

Conclusion: no proven HDR fix yet. Forcing VSync would be a latency/tearing
tradeoff, not a demonstrated correction to the reported defect. Game HDR,
VSync, resolution, and quality settings remain untouched. The next useful
diagnostic would be an actual HDR/SDR capture of the same game scene with
GPU completion, drawable presentation, and refresh timing correlated.
That was not performed in this MoltenVK investigation. Later user tests
used a separate experimental KosmicKrisp branch; they do not validate MoltenVK.

source, two minimal shader inputs and embedded SPIR-V are in

the targeted tests. Full Vulkan CTS, broad tessellation coverage, a dedicated
secondary-command hoisting reproducer and subpass GPU coverage were not run.

is included in this publication. Nothing here fixes HDR pacing or removes
the fork's remaining deliberate rendering/precision compromises.
